about

Built by someone who was
tired of being the bus

AntLegion is designed and built by Carter.Yang (@YangKGcsdms) — one architect, one axiom: facts, not commands. It exists because running several AI agents on one project kept turning the human into the coordination medium, relaying state between windows.

the story

A second system, on purpose

The first attempt — claw_fact_bus (2026-03, Python) — made the bus an arbiter that pushed facts to interested agents. It died of exactly the diseases this design cures: server-side state, implicit commands, coordination rules living in the runtime.

The rewrite deleted everything except what cannot be deleted — the total order — and moved every meaning into reader-side folds. The result is a stateless bus whose spec fits in one document, with exactly-once claiming as a theorem rather than a lock. EVOLUTION.md tells the whole v0 → v1 → v2 story; building the failed version first is why this one is shaped like this.

The lineage is older than AI: on a car's CAN bus (1986), no central computer commands the control units. AntLegion keeps that broadcast discipline and adds the two things CAN never had — memory and causality. Worker units are called DCUs, in honor of ECUs.
